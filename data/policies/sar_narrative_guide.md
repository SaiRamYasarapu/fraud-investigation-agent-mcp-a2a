# Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) Narrative Guide

*Northbridge Bank (fictional). Synthetic policy written for a demo project.*

## Purpose
A SAR narrative explains to regulators why activity is suspicious. It must stand on its own and be clear to someone who has never seen the account.

## Structure: the five Ws and how
1. **Who** is involved: the customer, counterparties and any linked accounts.
2. **What** happened: the transactions, amounts and instruments.
3. **When**: the dates and time span.
4. **Where**: branches, channels, countries and banks involved.
5. **Why** it is suspicious: the specific indicators, linked to known typologies.
6. **How** the activity was carried out, if known.

## Writing rules
- Use plain, factual language. Avoid guesses and labels such as "criminal".
- State the evidence for each claim.
- Reference the internal case number and the alerts that triggered review.
- Include actions taken, such as account restrictions or closure.

## Timing
File within 30 days of first detecting the suspicious activity.

## Confidentiality
The existence of a SAR must never be disclosed to the customer.
